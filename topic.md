# Doctoral study topic
## **Topic title**
Monitoring methods for amphibian species of European importance in small water bodies and the assessment of their population status in the Czech Natura 2000 network

## **Brief description and objectives** 
The research will focus on a critical evaluation of traditional and novel methods for monitoring the status of amphibian populations and habitats, including their applicability for practical nature conservation. This will include a comparison of the detection success of environmental DNA and passive acoustic monitoring against conventional field methods (funnel trapping, visual and nocturnal counts). The aim is to test the reliability of modern methods for screening habitat quality and the presence of flagship species of Annex II of the Habitats Directive, particularly the great crested newt (*Triturus cristatus*).

The main output will be scientific publications containing the results of the method comparison and resulting recommendations for practical use in biological assessment, as well as determining the limits of both approaches when monitoring Natura 2000 network sites. Another output will be an evaluation of the status of amphibians in the Czech Natura 2000 network after seven years of standardised data collection, including an analysis of the relationship between the identified population trends, land cover, and land use in the surrounding area.
